# Decisions: Creation tools

Newest first. See the [index](../decisions.md).

## 2026-10-15
- **bodyA is chosen by joint distance to `rootBody`, counted over undirected joints; unlinked bodies count as farthest, and ties go to the body earlier in `bodies` order.** Creating a joint never reorients existing joints. The rule lives in `orderJointBodies` (`js/model/queries.js`), used by the Hinge tool; `createJoint` takes bodyA and bodyB already ordered and never reorders. Reason: DESIGN said "closer to `rootBody`" without a metric or a tie rule, and most joints are drawn before their limb is linked (both unlinked); `bodies` order is deterministic, model-only, and matches the usual build order from the torso outward and the hit order (later bodies are on top, so the topmost shape usually becomes the child). Counting joints in either direction keeps the tree connected when a user has swapped a joint in the inspector. Not a format change. (DESIGN §2.4, §5.5; docs/architecture.md, Joint graph)
- **`js/model/queries.js` is created by #16 with `jointTree`, `jointBetween` and `orderJointBodies`;** `jointTree` is a breadth-first search from `rootBody` that visits edges in `joints` order, so depths are shortest joint counts and the spanning tree, and hence which joint is a loop joint, is deterministic. Loop joints are only reported among bodies reachable from `rootBody`. Reason: the Hinge tool needs depths before #13 or #19 land; BFS gives the "closer" metric directly; an unlinked part is already reported as unlinked, and flagging its internal cycles as loops would double-report it. (DESIGN §2.4; docs/architecture.md, Joint graph, Module map)
- **The Hinge tool refuses a press with fewer than two bodies under the pointer, or on a pair already joined in either order; a joint that closes a longer loop is allowed and becomes a loop joint.** A refused press changes nothing and is signalled beforehand by a `not-allowed` cursor while hovering. The same-pair refusal is also a `createJoint` precondition. Reason: owner priority is that joining two bodies works reliably; a second hinge between the same pair is never useful (two anchors weld them, one anchor is redundant) and usually comes from a repeated or double click, while longer loops (two hands on one object) are valid physics that DESIGN already supports with a warning. The cursor is the only pre-press feedback because tools receive no pointer-leave event, so an idle overlay preview would stay behind when the pointer leaves the viewport. (DESIGN §5.5; docs/architecture.md, Tools, Commands and undo)
- **Hinge bodies come from the pointer position, the anchor is the grid-snapped press point.** Reason: the bodies the user sees under the cursor are the ones joined; hit testing at the snapped point could pick other bodies, or none, at a spot the user did not point at. A snapped anchor outside both shapes is reported by the existing validation warning. (DESIGN §5.4, §5.5)
- **Hinge limit drag: θ is the screen distance from the anchor's current screen position, 1 px = 1°, rounded to whole degrees, clamped to 0–180°, SHIFT rounds to 15°; a drag ending at θ = 0 creates a pin; a click creates a pin.** The press uses the same 4 px drag latch as Circle and Box. Reason: whole degrees give clean inspector values; measuring from the anchor's current screen position keeps θ equal to the drawn arc after a pan or zoom mid-drag; SHIFT already means 15° angle snap everywhere else; a zero-width enabled limit would lock the joint, which nobody means by dragging back. (DESIGN §5.4, §5.5; docs/architecture.md, Tools)
- **The Hinge preview is the joint gizmo of §5.4 in the hover color (circle, needle along the zero direction, arc once θ > 0), computed with `jointZeroAngle` from world values.** Reason: the needle points to bodyB's origin, so the user sees which body becomes the child before releasing, and the preview is exactly the gizmo the joint gets after creation. (DESIGN §5.4, §5.5; docs/architecture.md, Tools)
- **`createJoint({ bodyA, bodyB, anchor, limits? })` in `js/commands/create.js` takes body uids and a root-local anchor, appends one joint with one `add` op, leaves `rootBody` untouched, and returns the joint uid.** Preconditions (non-body or equal uids, an already joined pair, a non-finite anchor, limits not around 0) throw before an id is allocated. Reason: same shape as `createShapeBody`, exact undo and redo through the `add` inverse; uids match the rest of the command API, and the command stores file ids as references are stored. (DESIGN §2.4; docs/architecture.md, Commands and undo; docs/schema.md)
- **Circle and Box: a click without a drag creates the shape at its schema default size (circle radius 0.25 m, box 0.5 × 0.5 m) centered at the snapped click point, whatever modifiers are held; with grid snap on, the dragged circle radius is rounded to the minor spacing (`snapLength`), never below it.** A press becomes a drag once the pointer has moved 4 px from the press point and stays one until release; a drag that ends with a size of 0 is refused. Reason: owner decisions for #14; a click must give a usable shape instead of a refused zero-size one, and latching the drag keeps a drag that comes back near the press from turning into a default shape. The radius is snapped as a length, not as a snapped pointer, so it lands on multiples of the step. (DESIGN §5.4, §5.5; docs/architecture.md, Tools)
- **Box ALT enforces symmetry in both axes, the same effect as CTRL: the box is centered on the anchor; ALT and CTRL together are the same as either; SHIFT still makes the final box square.** Sizes and centers follow one per-axis rule with an example in §5.5. Reason: owner decision for #14; DESIGN gave ALT "symmetry" and CTRL "anchor at the center", and the owner confirmed they are the same operation. This replaces the earlier interpretation that centered only the drag's shorter axis under ALT. (DESIGN §5.5; docs/architecture.md, Tools)
- **`createShapeBody({ position, angle, shape }, label?)` in a new `js/commands/create.js` is the one command for shape creation;** it appends the body with its fixture in a single `add` op, sets `rootBody` when it is `null`, names objects after their id numbers (`body_<n>`, `<type>_<n>`), validates its arguments before allocating ids, and returns the shape uid. Tools convert world drafts to root-local data (`toLocal` of the snapped center, body angle = −root angle). Reason: one undo step and one `change` per creation, exact undo and redo through the existing `add`/`set` inverses, and the same command serves Polygon (#15); keeping frames out of the command keeps it free of camera and snapping. (DESIGN §2.1, §2.2; docs/architecture.md, Commands and undo, Module map; docs/schema.md)
- **Circle and Box share a core factory, `dragShapeTool` in `js/tools/drag-shape.js`, with their draft geometry exported as pure functions (`circleDraft`, `boxDraft`).** The preview uses only `circle`, `rect`, `fill` and `stroke` of the handed `PIXI.Graphics`, in `COLORS.highlight`. Reason: the two tools differ only in their geometry; Polygon (#15) has a multi-click lifecycle and does not use the factory. Pure draft functions make the modifier table testable without events, and a fixed Graphics surface lets tests record drawing with a fake. (docs/architecture.md, Tools, Module map)
